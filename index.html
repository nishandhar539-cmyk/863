01

Public Speaking & Presentations

Key Phrases

  • Good morning, everyone. — সবাইকে শুভ সকাল।
  • Today, I'm going to talk about ... — আজ আমি ... নিয়ে কথা বলব।
  • Let me begin with ... — শুরু করছি ... দিয়ে।
  • Moving on to my next point ... — এবার আমার পরবর্তী বিষয়ে আসি।
  • In conclusion, ... — পরিশেষে বলতে চাই ...
  • Thank you for listening. — শোনার জন্য ধন্যবাদ।

Example

Good morning, everyone. Today, I'm going to talk about the importance of tree plantation. Let me begin with a simple fact: trees give us oxygen. In conclusion, every one of us should plant at least one tree this year. Thank you for listening.

Speaking Tips

  • Make eye contact with your audience instead of reading word-for-word.
  • Pause for a second between sentences — it sounds more confident than rushing.
  • Practice your speech out loud at least three times before presenting it.

Practice Exercise

  • Prepare a 1-minute self-introduction speech using at least three of the key phrases above.
  • Record yourself giving the speech and listen back to check your pace.

02

Job & Scholarship Interviews

Key Phrases

  • Tell me about yourself. — নিজের সম্পর্কে বলুন।
  • What are your strengths? — আপনার শক্তির দিক কী?
  • I am hardworking and a quick learner. — আমি পরিশ্রমী এবং দ্রুত শিখি।
  • Could you tell me more about ...? — আপনি কি আরেকটু বলবেন ... সম্পর্কে?
  • Thank you for this opportunity. — এই সুযোগের জন্য ধন্যবাদ।

Example

Interviewer: Tell me about yourself.
Candidate: I am currently in Class Ten, and I am hardworking and a quick learner. I enjoy science subjects and I'm applying for this scholarship to continue my studies. Thank you for this opportunity.

Speaking Tips

  • Speak slowly and clearly — nervousness often makes people rush their words.
  • Avoid one-word answers; always add a short reason or example.
  • Sit straight, make eye contact, and smile naturally.

Practice Exercise

  • Prepare spoken answers to "Tell me about yourself" and "What are your strengths?" and say them out loud.
  • Ask a friend or family member to play interviewer and ask you these two questions.

03

Debate & Discussion English

Key Phrases

  • I agree with you, but ... — আমি আপনার সাথে একমত, কিন্তু ...
  • In my opinion, ... — আমার মতে, ...
  • That's a valid point, however ... — এটা যুক্তিসঙ্গত কথা, তবে ...
  • Let's look at it from another angle. — এটাকে অন্যভাবে দেখা যাক।
  • To sum up my argument, ... — আমার যুক্তি সংক্ষেপে বলতে গেলে, ...

Example

A: I think mobile phones should be banned in class.
B: I agree with you, but in my opinion, they can also help students research quickly during lessons.

Speaking Tips

  • Stay calm and polite, even when you strongly disagree.
  • Always support your opinion with at least one clear reason.
  • Use "however" or "but" to disagree politely instead of interrupting.

Practice Exercise

  • Pick a topic — such as "social media is good for students" — and argue both sides out loud.
  • Practice disagreeing politely using "I agree with you, but ..." three times today.

04

Idioms for Fluent Speaking

Key Phrases

  • Break the ice — শুরুতে জড়তা কাটানো
  • Piece of cake — খুব সহজ কাজ
  • Under the weather — অসুস্থ বোধ করা
  • Hit the books — মন দিয়ে পড়াশোনা করা
  • Once in a blue moon — কালেভদ্রে, খুব কম ঘটে এমন

Example

A: This exam looks really hard.
B: Don't worry — it'll be a piece of cake for you if you hit the books this week.

Speaking Tips

  • Don't translate idioms word-for-word into Bangla — learn the full meaning instead.
  • Try using one new idiom in a real sentence every day.
  • Listen for idioms when you watch English videos or shows.

Practice Exercise

  • Use three idioms from the list above in your own sentences today.
  • Explain the meaning of "under the weather" to a friend without translating it directly.

05

Giving Opinions Confidently

Key Phrases

  • I personally believe ... — আমি ব্যক্তিগতভাবে মনে করি ...
  • From my point of view, ... — আমার দৃষ্টিকোণ থেকে, ...
  • I'm not entirely convinced that ... — আমি পুরোপুরি নিশ্চিত নই যে ...
  • I'd say that ... — আমি বলব যে ...
  • It seems to me that ... — আমার কাছে মনে হয় যে ...

Example

A: What do you think about online classes?
B: Personally, I believe they're useful for revision, but it seems to me that nothing beats a real classroom.

Speaking Tips

  • Avoid just saying "I think so" — expand your answer with a short reason.
  • Use linking words like "because" and "so" to connect your ideas.
  • It's fine to say you're unsure — use "I'm not entirely convinced that ..." instead of staying silent.

Practice Exercise

  • Give your opinion on "social media" out loud, using two of the phrases above.
  • Ask a classmate their opinion on a topic and respond using "From my point of view, ..."

06

Advanced Small Talk

Key Phrases

  • How's everything going? — সবকিছু কেমন চলছে?
  • Long time no see! — অনেকদিন দেখা হয়নি!
  • What have you been up to? — তুমি কী নিয়ে ব্যস্ত ছিলে?
  • That reminds me of ... — এটা আমাকে ... এর কথা মনে করিয়ে দিচ্ছে।
  • Anyway, I should get going. — যাই হোক, আমাকে এখন যেতে হবে।

Example

A: Long time no see! How's everything going?
B: Pretty good, actually. What have you been up to?
A: Just studying for exams. Anyway, I should get going — see you soon!

Speaking Tips

  • Ask a follow-up question instead of giving one-word replies.
  • Keep the tone light, friendly and relaxed.
  • Use "anyway" naturally when you want to end or change the topic politely.

Practice Exercise

  • Start a short small-talk conversation with a classmate using two phrases above.
  • Practice ending a conversation politely using "Anyway, I should get going."

07

Spoken Test Prep

Key Phrases

  • Could you repeat the question, please? — প্রশ্নটি আবার বলবেন কি?
  • That's an interesting question. — এটা একটা আকর্ষণীয় প্রশ্ন।
  • Let me think about that for a moment. — একটু ভাবতে দিন।
  • To be honest, ... — সত্যি বলতে, ...
  • I haven't thought about it before, but ... — আগে এটা নিয়ে ভাবিনি, তবে ...

Example

Examiner: Describe your favourite place.
Student: That's an interesting question — let me think for a moment. My favourite place is my village, because it's calm and I can see green fields everywhere.

Speaking Tips

  • Never stay silent — use a filler phrase like "Let me think about that" while you plan your answer.
  • Speak in full sentences, not short fragments.
  • Practice describing everyday objects and places for one full minute without stopping.

Practice Exercise

  • Set a timer for 1 minute and describe your school without stopping.
  • Practice answering "Describe your favourite place" using at least two key phrases above.

08

Storytelling in English

Key Phrases

  • Once upon a time, ... — এক সময়, ...
  • Suddenly, ... — হঠাৎ করে, ...
  • After that, ... — এরপর, ...
  • In the end, ... — শেষ পর্যন্ত, ...
  • The moral of the story is ... — গল্পের শিক্ষা হলো ...

Example

Once upon a time, there was a poor farmer. Suddenly, he found a bag of gold coins on the road. After that, he returned it to its owner. In the end, the owner rewarded his honesty. The moral of the story is that honesty is always rewarded.

Speaking Tips

  • Use past tense consistently while telling a story.
  • Use linking words — then, after that, finally — to show the order of events.
  • Change your tone and pace to make the story more exciting.

Practice Exercise

  • Tell a short story about your day using at least three of the linking words above.
  • Practice the farmer story out loud, focusing on clear past-tense verbs.

Parts of Speech

The Rule

  • Every English word does one of 8 jobs in a sentence: Noun, Pronoun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb, Preposition, Conjunction, Interjection.
  • Noun — names a person, place or thing. (Rahim, Dhaka, book)
  • Pronoun — replaces a noun. (he, she, it, they)
  • Verb — shows an action or state. (run, is, have)
  • Adjective — describes a noun. (tall, red, honest)
  • Adverb — describes a verb, adjective or another adverb. (quickly, very)

Example

The clever (adjective) student (noun) solved (verb) the sum quickly (adverb).

Common Mistake

  • Mixing up adjective and adverb: say “She sings beautifully”, not “She sings beautiful”.

Practice Exercise

  • Identify the parts of speech: “The old man walked slowly to the market.”
  • Write one sentence using a noun, a verb and an adjective together.

Tense

The Rule

  • Present Simple: Subject + verb (s/es) — I play, He plays.
  • Present Continuous: am/is/are + verb+ing — She is reading.
  • Past Simple: Subject + verb (past form) — They went home.
  • Past Continuous: was/were + verb+ing — He was sleeping at 9pm.
  • Future Simple: will + verb — We will meet tomorrow.
  • Perfect tenses use have/has/had + past participle — I have finished my homework.

Example

By the time the bell rang, the students had finished (past perfect) their exam.

Common Mistake

  • Don't mix “since” and “for” — use since for a point in time (since 2020) and for for a duration (for two years).

Practice Exercise

  • Fill in the blank: She ___ (live) in Dhaka since 2015.
  • Change to future tense: “He completes the assignment.”

✅

Right Forms of Verbs

The Rule

  • Choose the verb form that matches the subject, tense and structure of the sentence.
  • After “to”, always use the base form: to go, not to going.
  • After a preposition, use the verb+ing form: fond of reading, not fond of read.
  • Modal verbs (can, could, may, must, should) are always followed by the base form: must finish, not must to finish.

Example

He is interested in learning (not learn) English. He must submit (not submits) his homework on time.

Common Mistake

  • Writing “He can sings well” instead of the correct “He can sing well.”

Practice Exercise

  • Fill in the blank with the right form of the verb: I enjoy ___ (play) cricket in the evening.
  • Correct the sentence: “She should to finish her paragraph first.”

🔗

Subject–Verb Agreement

The Rule

  • A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.
  • Two nouns joined by “and” usually take a plural verb: Rahim and Karim are here.
  • Words like “each”, “every”, “either”, “neither” take a singular verb.
  • A collective noun (team, family, class) usually takes a singular verb when acting as one unit.

Example

Each of the students has (not have) submitted the assignment. The team is playing well this season.

Common Mistake

  • Ignoring words between subject and verb: “The list of items is long” — not “are”, since “list” is the real subject.

Practice Exercise

  • Choose the correct verb: “Neither of the answers ___ (is/are) correct.”
  • Correct the sentence: “The number of students have increased this year.”
↑ Back to list
🔄

Voice: Active & Passive

The Rule

  • Active: Subject does the action. The cat chased the mouse.
  • Passive: Subject receives the action. The mouse was chased by the cat.
  • Passive formula: object + be (correct tense) + past participle + by + subject.
  • The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.

Example

Active: The teacher explains the lesson.
Passive: The lesson is explained by the teacher.

Common Mistake

  • Forgetting to change the tense of “be” to match the original verb's tense.

Practice Exercise

  • Change to passive voice: “The workers built the bridge.”
  • Change to active voice: “The cake was baked by my mother.”
↑ Back to list
💬

Narration

The Rule

  • Direct speech reports the exact words in quotation marks: He said, "I am tired."
  • Indirect speech reports the meaning without quotation marks, often shifting the tense back: He said that he was tired.
  • Pronouns change to match the speaker: I → he/she, we → they.
  • Time and place words shift: now → then, today → that day, here → there.

Example

Direct: She said, "I will go there tomorrow."
Indirect: She said that she would go there the next day.

Common Mistake

  • Forgetting to remove the quotation marks and the word "that" for questions — questions use if/whether instead.

Practice Exercise

  • Change to indirect speech: He said, "I am reading a book."
  • Change to indirect speech: She asked, "Are you coming to the fair?"
↑ Back to list
🔤

Articles

The Rule

  • Use a before a consonant sound: a book, a university (starts with a "yoo" sound).
  • Use an before a vowel sound: an apple, an hour (the "h" is silent).
  • Use the for a specific or already-known noun: the sun, the boy I met yesterday.
  • No article is needed before plural or uncountable nouns used in a general sense: Rice is our staple food.

Example

I saw an owl sitting on the tree near a pond.

Common Mistake

  • Writing "an university" instead of the correct "a university", since "university" starts with a consonant sound.

Practice Exercise

  • Fill in the blanks: ___ honest man never tells ___ lie.
  • Correct the sentence: "She is a MP of our area."
↑ Back to list
📍

Prepositions

The Rule

  • A preposition shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and another word — usually place, time or direction.
  • in — a month, year or enclosed space: in June, in the box.
  • on — a day or date, or a surface: on Friday, on the table.
  • at — a specific time or point: at 5pm, at the door.

Example

We will meet at the station on Friday in the morning.

Common Mistake

  • Saying "married with" instead of the correct "married to".

Practice Exercise

  • Fill in the blanks: He is good ___ Math but weak ___ English.
  • Correct the sentence: "I am interested about painting."
↑ Back to list
⚖️

Degree of Comparison

The Rule

  • Positive: no comparison — Rahim is tall.
  • Comparative: compares two — add -er or "more": Rahim is taller than Karim.
  • Superlative: compares three or more — add -est or "most": Rahim is the tallest boy in class.
  • To change comparative to positive: "No other boy in class is as tall as Rahim."

Example

Positive: Dhaka is a big city.
Comparative: Dhaka is bigger than Rajshahi.
Superlative: Dhaka is the biggest city in Bangladesh.

Common Mistake

  • Doubling the comparison: "more taller" is wrong — use either "more" or "-er", never both.

Practice Exercise

  • Change to superlative degree: "Very few boys in the class are as intelligent as Rakib."
  • Change to positive degree: "Iron is more useful than any other metal."
↑ Back to list
🔀

Conditional Sentences

The Rule

  • Zero conditional (general truth): If + present simple, present simple. If you heat ice, it melts.
  • First conditional (real future possibility): If + present simple, will + verb. If it rains, we will stay home.
  • Second conditional (unreal present/future): If + past simple, would + verb. If I had money, I would buy a car.
  • Third conditional (unreal past): If + past perfect, would have + past participle. If I had studied, I would have passed.

Example

If she works hard, she will succeed in the exam.

Common Mistake

  • Using "will" in the if-clause: "If it will rain" is wrong — say "If it rains."

Practice Exercise

  • Fill in the blank: If I ___ (be) you, I would apologise.
  • Complete the sentence: "If he had come earlier, ___."
↑ Back to list
✍️

Completing Sentences

The Rule

  • Read the clue clause carefully — it tells you whether the missing part should be affirmative, negative or a reason.
  • "As soon as" is usually followed by another action happening immediately after.
  • "Unless" means "if not" — the completing clause should not repeat "not" again.
  • Keep the tense of your completion consistent with the given clause.

Example

As soon as the bell rang, the students rushed out of the classroom.

Common Mistake

  • Writing "Unless you don't work hard, you will fail" — the double negative is wrong; say "Unless you work hard, you will fail."

Practice Exercise

  • Complete: "No sooner had he reached the station than ___."
  • Complete: "Had I known about the exam, ___."
↑ Back to list
🔁

Transformation of Sentences

The Rule

  • The meaning must stay exactly the same — only the structure changes.
  • Assertive to negative: "Everybody knows it." → "Nobody is ignorant of it."
  • Simple to complex: "Being tired, he sat down." → "As he was tired, he sat down."
  • Complex to compound: "As he was late, he missed the bus." → "He was late, so he missed the bus."

Example

Interrogative: "Isn't he honest?" → Assertive: "He is honest."

Common Mistake

  • Changing the actual meaning of the sentence while transforming — always re-read to check the meaning is unchanged.

Practice Exercise

  • Change to negative: "He is the best student in class."
  • Change to complex sentence: "Working hard, he passed the exam."
↑ Back to list
❗

Punctuation & Capitalization

The Rule

  • Start every sentence with a capital letter and end it with a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark.
  • Use a comma to separate items in a list and before a coordinating conjunction joining two independent clauses.
  • Capitalize proper nouns: names of people, places, days, months and languages.
  • Use an apostrophe for possession (Rahim's book) and contractions (don't).

Example

Rahim, Karim, and Salim went to Dhaka on Friday, but they returned the same evening.

Common Mistake

  • Forgetting the comma before "and" in a list of three or more items.

Practice Exercise

  • Punctuate: "have you finished your homework asked the teacher"
  • Correct the capitalization: "we visited cox's bazar last january."

01

Basic Greetings

Simple English & Bangla Meaning

  • Hello / Hi — হ্যালো / হাই
  • Good morning — শুভ সকাল
  • How are you? — তুমি কেমন আছো?
  • I am fine, thank you. — আমি ভালো আছি, ধন্যবাদ।
  • Good night — শুভ রাত্রি
  • See you later. — পরে দেখা হবে।

Example

A: Hello! How are you?
B: I am fine, thank you. And you?
A: I am fine too. See you later!

Pronunciation Tips

  • Say “How” clearly — it sounds like “হাউ”, not “হো”.
  • Give a little stress on the word “fine” when you answer.

Practice Exercise

  • Greet your teacher in English tomorrow morning.
  • Ask a friend “How are you?” and answer their question back.

02

Self Introduction

Simple English & Bangla Meaning

  • My name is ... — আমার নাম ...
  • I am a student. — আমি একজন ছাত্র/ছাত্রী।
  • I study in Class Seven. — আমি সপ্তম শ্রেণিতে পড়ি।
  • I live in ... — আমি ... এ থাকি।
  • I am from Bangladesh. — আমি বাংলাদেশ থেকে এসেছি।
  • My hobby is ... — আমার শখ হলো ...

Example

A: What is your name?
B: My name is Rafiq.
A: Which class do you study in?
B: I study in Class Seven.

Pronunciation Tips

  • “Student” is said as “স্টুডেন্ট” — stress the first part, “STU”.
  • Say your name clearly and slowly so people can understand it.

Practice Exercise

  • Write 3 sentences introducing yourself.
  • Practice saying your introduction out loud in front of a mirror.

03

Daily Conversations

Simple English & Bangla Meaning

  • What are you doing? — তুমি কী করছ?
  • I am reading a book. — আমি একটি বই পড়ছি।
  • Have you eaten? — তুমি কি খেয়েছ?
  • What time is it? — এখন কয়টা বাজে?
  • I am going home. — আমি বাড়ি যাচ্ছি।

Example

A: What are you doing?
B: I am doing my homework.
A: Okay, see you later!

Pronunciation Tips

  • “Doing” is pronounced “ডুইং”, not “ডোয়িং”.

Practice Exercise

  • Ask a family member “What are you doing?” today and note their reply.
  • Tell someone what you are doing right now, in one English sentence.

04

Classroom English

Simple English & Bangla Meaning

  • May I come in, sir/ma'am? — আমি কি ভিতরে আসতে পারি স্যার/ম্যাডাম?
  • May I go to the toilet? — আমি কি টয়লেটে যেতে পারি?
  • I don't understand. — আমি বুঝতে পারছি না।

Pronunciation Tips

Simple English & Bangla Meaning

  • think (not “sink”) — চিন্তা করা — জিভ দাঁতের ফাঁকে রাখতে হয়

  • walk (l is silent) — হাঁটা — “wok” এর মতো শোনা যায়
  • important (stress on 2nd part) — গুরুত্বপূর্ণ — imPORtant

Pronunciation Tips

  • Make the “th” sound by placing your tongue between your teeth, not just saying “t”.
  • Some English words have silent letters — “know”, “walk”, “hour”.
  • Always stress the correct syllable — say “imPORtant”, not “IMportant”.

Importance of Education
01

A School Library

A school library is a special room full of books, magazines and newspapers for students to read. It is one of the most useful places in a school. Students can borrow story books, science books and reference books from the library. A librarian looks after the books and helps students find what they need. Reading in the library builds good habits and improves our knowledge. It is usually a quiet place, so students can read or study without any noise. A good school library also keeps computers and dictionaries for research. Every school should have a well-stocked library, because it helps students grow into better learners. We should use the library often and take good care of the books we borrow.

↑ Back to list
02

Tree Plantation

Tree plantation means planting trees in an open place such as a field, roadside or garden. Trees give us oxygen, food, wood and shade. They also stop soil erosion and keep the environment cool. Bangladesh is losing many trees because of housing and industry, so tree plantation is very important for us today. The government celebrates a Tree Fair every year to encourage people to plant more trees. Students, farmers and social workers should plant trees on empty land near their homes and schools. One person can plant many trees over a lifetime, and this small effort can make a big difference. If everyone plants at least one tree, our country will become greener and healthier. We must protect young trees from being cut down.

↑ Back to list
03

A Rainy Day

A rainy day is a day when it rains continuously from morning till night. The sky remains cloudy and dark, and the sun cannot be seen at all. On such a day, roads become muddy and slippery, and it is hard to go outside. Rickshaw pullers and daily workers suffer the most, since they cannot earn money easily. Students often cannot go to school, and classes may be cancelled. However, a rainy day also has its own beauty. Children enjoy playing in the rain, and farmers are happy because the rain is good for their crops. Sitting near a window with a cup of tea and a book is a favourite pastime on such a day. Overall, a rainy day brings both hardship and happiness at the same time.

↑ Back to list
04

A Winter Morning

A winter morning is usually cold, foggy and dull. In Bangladesh, winter comes in the months of Poush and Magh. On a winter morning, the sun rises late and its light is weak. Thick fog covers fields and roads, making it hard to see far away. People wrap themselves in warm clothes, shawls and blankets to protect themselves from the cold. Village people light fires early in the morning to keep themselves warm. Farmers still go to work in the fields despite the cold weather. Winter mornings are also famous for fresh vegetables, date juice and delicious pitha, a traditional Bangladeshi sweet. Although it feels uncomfortable to leave a warm bed, a winter morning has its own simple charm that many people enjoy.

05

My Best Friend

A best friend is a person we trust and love the most, besides our family. My best friend's name is Rafiq, and we study in the same class. He is honest, helpful and always ready to support me in difficult times. We share our books, tiffin and secrets with each other without any hesitation. Whenever I face a problem in my studies, he explains it to me patiently. We play together, go to school together and often do our homework together after school. A true friend never leaves us alone, whether we are happy or sad. My best friend has taught me the value of honesty and kindness. I feel lucky to have such a good friend in my life, and I hope our friendship will last forever.

06

My School

My school is a place where I learn, play and grow every day with my friends. It has a large building with many classrooms, a library, a science laboratory and a big playground. My school was established many years ago and has a good reputation in our area. The teachers are kind, experienced and always ready to help students understand their lessons. Along with studies, my school also arranges sports, cultural programmes and debate competitions. Discipline is strictly maintained, and students are taught to respect their teachers and elders. I have made many good friends here, and every day brings a new experience. My school has shaped my character and given me the knowledge I need for the future. I am proud to be a student of my school.

07

Physical Exercise

Physical exercise means any activity that keeps our body active and healthy, such as walking, running, swimming or playing games. Regular exercise strengthens our muscles, improves blood circulation and keeps our heart healthy. It also helps us stay fit, reduces stress and improves our mood. Students who exercise regularly can concentrate better on their studies and stay away from many diseases. Simple exercises like morning walks, cycling or playing football for thirty minutes a day are enough to keep the body fit. Unfortunately, many students today spend too much time on mobile phones instead of playing outside. We should make physical exercise a daily habit from an early age. A healthy body always leads to a healthy and active mind, so we must never ignore the importance of exercise.

↑ Back to list
08

Early Rising

Early rising means waking up early in the morning, usually before sunrise. It is considered one of the best habits a person can develop. When we rise early, we get fresh air, peace and enough time to prepare for the day ahead. Early risers can study, exercise or help their family before going to school or work. The morning atmosphere is calm and pollution-free, which is good for both body and mind. Many successful people in the world have followed this simple habit throughout their lives. Waking up early also helps us go to bed on time, which improves our overall health. On the other hand, people who wake up late often feel tired and rush through their work. Therefore, students should try to build the habit of early rising from a young age.

↑ Back to list
09

A Book Fair

A book fair is a special event where many publishers and writers display their books for readers to see and buy. In Bangladesh, the most famous book fair is the Ekushey Boi Mela, held every February at the Bangla Academy in Dhaka to honour the Language Movement. Thousands of visitors, including students, teachers and book lovers, come to the fair every day. New books on stories, poems, science and history are published especially for this occasion. Many writers also come to meet their readers and sign copies of their books. A book fair encourages people, especially children, to develop a habit of reading. It also gives young writers a chance to introduce their work to the public. Visiting a book fair is both an enjoyable and educational experience for every student.

↑ Back to list
10

Student Life

Student life is the period when a person studies in a school, college or university to gain knowledge and prepare for the future. It is considered the best and most important part of a person's life. During student life, we learn discipline, responsibility and the value of hard work. Along with studies, students should also take part in sports, cultural activities and social work to build their character. This stage of life is free from many worries and responsibilities, so it should be used wisely to build a strong foundation. Good habits formed during student life, such as punctuality and honesty, stay with a person forever. Students should respect their teachers, listen carefully in class and complete their homework regularly. A well-spent student life leads to a successful and meaningful future.

↑ Back to list
11

Digital Bangladesh

Digital Bangladesh is a vision to use modern technology, especially computers and the internet, to improve education, healthcare, agriculture and government services across the country. Under this plan, many government offices now offer online services, so people do not need to visit offices in person for simple work. Students can now access online classes, digital libraries and educational videos from their homes. Farmers get weather updates and market prices on their mobile phones, which helps them plan better. Mobile banking and online payment systems have also made daily transactions easier and faster for everyone. However, Digital Bangladesh also requires internet access and digital knowledge for all citizens, including those in rural areas. If used properly, digital technology can bring rapid progress to our education, economy and daily life. Every citizen should learn basic digital skills to benefit from this vision.

↑ Back to list
12

Environment Pollution

Environment pollution means the contamination of air, water and soil by harmful substances, which damages nature and harms living beings. In Bangladesh, factories release smoke and chemical waste into rivers, while vehicles emit harmful gases into the air every day. Plastic bags, polythene and other waste are thrown carelessly, which pollutes both land and water. Environment pollution causes serious health problems such as breathing difficulties, skin diseases and waterborne illnesses. It also destroys the habitats of animals, fish and plants, disturbing the natural balance of our ecosystem. To reduce pollution, we should avoid using polythene, plant more trees and dispose of waste properly in dustbins. Factories should also be required to treat their waste before releasing it into rivers. Everyone, from students to government officials, must work together to keep our environment clean and safe for future generations.

↑ Back to list
13

Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term changes in temperature and weather patterns on Earth, mostly caused by human activities. Burning fossil fuels, cutting down forests and increasing pollution release greenhouse gases that trap heat in the atmosphere. Bangladesh is one of the countries most affected by climate change, facing frequent floods, cyclones, rising sea levels and unpredictable rainfall. These changes threaten agriculture, damage homes and force many coastal people to leave their land permanently. Farmers often lose their crops due to sudden floods or long periods of drought caused by changing weather. To fight climate change, we need to plant more trees, save energy and reduce the use of plastic and fuel. Governments around the world must also work together to control pollution and protect the environment. Every small action we take today can help protect our planet for the future.

↑ Back to list
14

Traffic Jam

A traffic jam happens when too many vehicles gather on a road and cannot move forward smoothly. It is a very common and serious problem in the big cities of Bangladesh, especially in Dhaka. Traffic jams are caused by narrow roads, too many vehicles, illegal parking and a lack of proper traffic rules. People waste hours of their valuable time stuck in traffic every day while going to school, work or hospitals. It also increases air pollution, since vehicles release smoke while waiting for a long time in a jam. Students often reach school late, and office workers lose important working hours because of this problem. To reduce traffic jams, the government should build wider roads, improve public transport and strictly enforce traffic rules. Citizens should also follow traffic signals and avoid illegal parking to make our roads safer and smoother for everyone.

Food Adulteration

Food adulteration means mixing harmful or low-quality substances into food items to increase profit, which makes the food unsafe to eat. In Bangladesh, adulteration is found in many everyday foods, such as milk, fruits, vegetables, fish and sweets. Some dishonest traders use dangerous chemicals like formalin to keep fruits and fish fresh for a longer time. Eating adulterated food can cause serious diseases, including cancer, kidney problems and stomach illnesses, especially in children. This is a major public health problem that affects millions of people across the country every day. The government has laws against food adulteration, and mobile courts sometimes punish sellers who break these rules. However, strict and regular monitoring is still needed to stop this harmful practice completely. As responsible citizens, we should also raise our voice against food adulteration and buy food from trusted sources.

Reading Newspaper

A newspaper is a printed or online publication that gives us daily news about our country and the world. Reading a newspaper is a very useful habit that keeps us informed about politics, sports, education, business and important events. It also improves our vocabulary, general knowledge and reading skills, which are helpful for both studies and daily life. Students who read newspapers regularly can write better essays and speak more confidently about current affairs. Newspapers cover a wide range of topics, so readers can choose the sections that interest them the most. In today's digital age, many people now read newspapers on their mobile phones instead of paper copies. Reading the newspaper for a few minutes every day helps us become more aware and responsible citizens. Every student should try to develop this simple but valuable habit from an early age.

Your Aim in Life

Aim in life means the goal or purpose that a person wants to achieve in the future. Every person should have a clear aim in life, because it gives direction and meaning to our daily efforts. My aim in life is to become a doctor, so that I can serve sick and poor people in my country. Since childhood, I have been inspired by doctors who help patients recover and save lives. To achieve this goal, I am studying hard, especially in science subjects like biology and chemistry. I know that becoming a doctor requires years of study, patience and dedication, but I am ready to work hard for it. A person without an aim in life often feels lost and wastes valuable time and opportunities. Therefore, every student should choose a suitable aim early and work sincerely to achieve it step by step.

A Village Fair

A village fair is a traditional gathering held in rural areas, usually during a religious festival or a special season of the year. People from nearby villages come together to buy and sell various goods, such as toys, sweets, clothes and household items. A village fair is also a place of entertainment, with folk songs, puppet shows and traditional games for children and adults. Local craftsmen display handmade items like pottery, bamboo baskets and wooden toys, showing the rich culture of our villages. Food stalls sell delicious local sweets, snacks and drinks, which attract visitors of all ages. A village fair brings people together and strengthens social relationships within the community. It also helps to preserve our traditional culture and supports local craftsmen and small businesses. Visiting a village fair is always a joyful and memorable experience for everyone.

A Journey by Train

A journey by train is one of the most comfortable and enjoyable ways to travel from one place to another. Last year, I travelled from Dhaka to Rajshahi by train with my family, and it was truly a memorable experience. As the train moved forward, I saw green fields, rivers, small villages and busy stations passing by the window. Vendors walked through the train selling tea, snacks and cold drinks, adding to the excitement of the journey. Unlike a bus journey, a train journey allows passengers to walk around, talk with fellow travellers and enjoy the changing scenery comfortably. The gentle rocking motion of the train and the sound of its wheels created a relaxing atmosphere throughout the trip. We reached our destination safely, tired but happy after a wonderful journey. I will always remember this train journey as one of my favourite travel experiences.

Importance of Education

Education means gaining knowledge, skills and good values through learning, which helps a person grow and succeed in life. It is one of the most powerful tools for the development of both an individual and a nation. Education helps us understand the world better, think logically and make wise decisions in our daily life. A well-educated person can get a good job, support their family and contribute positively to society. Education also helps to reduce poverty, superstition and social problems, since educated people are more aware of their rights and duties. Bangladesh has been working hard to increase literacy rates and provide free education to all children up to a certain level. However, many children in remote areas still cannot access proper education due to poverty or lack of schools. Every child deserves quality education, because it is the key to building a bright and prosperous future for our country.
